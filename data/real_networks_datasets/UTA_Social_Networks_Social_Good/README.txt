METHODOLOGICAL INFORMATION

--------------------------

Research site:
The research was conducted at a large public university in the southern United States. 

Sample:
All 148 incoming Master of Social Work (MSW) students in the Fall 2014 class were invited to participate in the study.  Of the 145 students, 142 (97.9%) enrolled in the study.  

Students applied to the MSW program as either regular standing or advanced standing. The MSW program made eligibility determinations and enrolled students into one of the two tracks.  Regular standing students were required to complete 36 semester hours of credit to graduate with a MSW. Advanced standing students entered the program with a Bachelor of Social Work and could waive 25 credit hours in the MSW program.

The regular standing students were assigned to five cohorts – three full-time cohorts, two part-time cohorts – who took all of their classes together for the first semester of the program and then were integrated into the student body as a whole for the rest of their courses.  Advanced standing students were not assigned to a cohort; however, there was one course in the Fall 2014 semester that all the advanced standing students took together.  By wave 4, several students in the advanced standing track (identified as cohort 6) had graduated from the program and were not available to take the survey.  

Description of methods used for collection/generation of data: 
Survey data were collected from MSW students:
* at summer orientation prior to the beginning of the MSW program in July/August 2014 (wave 1); 
* eight weeks into the first semester of the program in October 2014 (wave 2); 
* at the end of the first semester in November/December 2014 (wave 3); and 
* at the end of the Fall 2015 semester in December 2015 (wave 4). 

The study was approved by the university's Institutional Review Board in two parts.  Waves 1-3 were part of the original study.  Wave 4 was added to the original study and required separate informed consent for participants.  Students not participating in wave 4 may have graduated or withdrawn from the program, or may remained in the program, but did not participate in the study. 

Data were collected using paper-and-pen surveys that were administered in group settings to classrooms of MSW students. Participants who were not in attendance during the classroom survey administration were invited to complete a survey in a private location up to one week after the group survey administration.  At any given wave, some students who were enrolled in the study did not complete a survey. 

Instruments assessing sense of belonging and cultural competency were administered in the surveys at wave 1, wave 3 and wave 4.  In addition, at wave 4, the survey questionnaire included survey items to assess perceived stress and an evaluation of the cohort experience. None of these items were measured at wave 2 of data collection which only collected social network information.  

Social network data were collected at all four waves of data collection. At wave 1, the participants answered an open-ended question requesting the names of other incoming students they knew.  At waves 2, 3, and 4, participants were provided with a roster of the names and cohort membership of the all the students in the program, including those who were not enrolled in the study. 

Archival data was collected from the school's admission office prior to wave 1 and after wave 4. At wave 1, archival data included student demographics, home address, incoming grade point average (GPA), and incoming GRE scores.  After wave 4, archival data consisted of course enrollment, grades, field evaluations for field placements up to Fall 2015, and GPAs each semester. Because wave 4 required separate informed consent, if a student did not re-enroll into the study at wave 4, these data will be missing. 

Methods for processing the data: 
The principal investigator (PI) used double data entry to enter the paper survey responses into Excel spreadsheets, compare the two versions, and reconcile discrepancies using the original survey responses.  Survey data were saved into two main types of files: characteristics and social network edgelists.  Archival data from the admissions office were appended to characteristics files.

Software- or Instrument-specific information needed to interpret the data, including software and hardware version numbers: 
CSV files are compatible with Microsoft Excel, Google Sheets, Numbers, and other software. 
Network analysis software, such as UCINET or Pajek can be used to interpret edgelists and matrix data.
